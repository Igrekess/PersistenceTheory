## The gesture

Marcel said that all of philosophy might fit into a single question:
*am I my body, or do I have a body?* Heidegger restates the same
tension differently (being against beings), Sartre differently again
(in-itself against for-itself). One dyad runs through Western thought:
**being** and **having**.

The aphorism that opens PT — *everything says what it is by revealing
what it no longer has* — turns precisely on that gap. What a thing
**is** shows itself in what it **does not have**, or has lost. Presence
is defined by dispossession.

This dyad is not just a metaphor. It is inscribed, almost literally, in
arithmetic.

## The integer as additive position

Our first relation to integers is positional: 1, 2, 3, 4… To count is
to add a unit, then another, then another. An integer is what it is by
its **place in the sequence**. It has no internal relation, no
decomposition — it simply *is*, at position *n*.

This is **additive arithmetic**: Σ ℤ, juxtaposition, succession,
quantified duration. The integer as **pure being**: 7 *is* the seventh
position, no more.

Addition preserves presence: 3 + 4 = 7 keeps the trace of the addends;
they stay accounted for in the result. The operation juxtaposes
without transforming.

## The integer as multiplicative factorisation

But the integer leads another life. 12 is not only *"the twelfth
position"* — it is also 2 × 2 × 3, that is, *"that which has two 2s
and one 3"*. Multiplication reveals an **internal structure**: parts,
constituents, a decomposition.

This is **multiplicative arithmetic**: Π primes, factorisation,
property, possession. The integer as **having**: 12 *has* two 2s and
one 3.

Multiplication, unlike addition, **destroys the positional trace** of
the factors in favour of their composition. 12 does not say whether
you reached it via 4 then ×3 or via 6 then ×2. It only states the
**library** of its primes.

## The fundamental theorem of arithmetic

Here is the pivot: *every integer greater than 1 admits one and only
one decomposition into a product of primes, up to ordering*.

In other words: **every being** (every additive position) **reveals a
unique having** (its factorisation). Unique factorisation is the
theorem that reconciles the two regimes.

This is exactly the opening sentence of PT, transposed from
philosophical to arithmetic language:

> *Everything says what it is* (its additive position)
> *by revealing what it no longer has* (its deferred factorisation —
> what was dispersed into factors).

The aphorism is not a metaphor. It is a literary gloss of the
fundamental theorem.

## Primes as fixed points

One special case lights everything up: the prime numbers.

A prime *p* has only one possible factorisation — itself. Its additive
position and its multiplicative content coincide. For a prime, **being
and having are identical**.

Primes are the only integers where the dyad collapses. They are the
**fixed points** of the passage between the two regimes — atoms of the
multiplicative, but also indecomposable additive positions.

That is why they are the **observable structures** of PT: what
persists under the sieve is what no longer decomposes, what *is*
exactly what it *has*.

## PT as cosmology of the dyad

The PT cascade — `s = 1/2 → {2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13}` — can then be reread
as a journey through the **cone of uncertainty between addition and
multiplication**. Each step of the sieve is a decision: what reduces
to a pure product (= persistent structure) survives; what only
extends an additive succession (= entropic dispersion) disappears.

The active backbone {2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13} is not an arbitrary choice
of numbers. It is the **set of positions where additive being and
multiplicative having coincide exactly** — where the dyad collapses,
where the constraint can no longer filter anything.

## A signature

Arithmetic thinks ontology. Ontology thinks arithmetic. This is not
a metaphor, not an analogy — it is the same structure, read at two
levels. PT takes that fact seriously and derives observables from it.

When you read *"reality exists in the cone of uncertainty between
addition and multiplication"*, you also read: *"reality exists in the
tension between being and having"*. The two sentences say the same
thing. And both describe what can, on passing through the filter,
persist without being lost.
